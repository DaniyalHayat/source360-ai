# Synthetic sourcing case

A procurement team receives disconnected ERP purchases, supplier aliases, invoice duplicates, mismatched bid units and contract snippets. The decision is whether to rebid a category and how to allocate a single annual RFQ among competing suppliers while respecting capacity and resilience policy.

The deterministic fixture has 100,000 source PO lines. Financially invalid or unmapped rows are quarantined. Published spend is approximately $261.4M across 36 months and ten categories. Off-contract spend and anomalous-price spend are review exposures, not assumed savings.

The IT Hardware RFQ assumes 8,000 each at an all-in incumbent reference of $984.40 per each ($7,875,200). Four bids are normalized to USD/EA, including a EUR BOX10 quote. The default balanced scenario uses a 60% concentration cap, at least two suppliers, at most three, 30% qualified allocation, and a $100 risk-unit-per-EA penalty. Results are generated by the optimizer; inspect the receipt rather than treating these assumptions as procurement policy.

The default fixture allocates 40% to Aster and 60% to Northstar, with $6,493,670 cash landed cost and $1,381,530 simulated savings (17.54%). The noncash risk preference adds approximately $296,466 to the optimization objective. These are model results on synthetic inputs, not a negotiated or realized saving. Monte Carlo bands are stress-cost illustrations and do not establish stressed capacity feasibility.

Recommended human actions: validate incumbent reference scope and currency, confirm quoted capacity and qualification, verify commercial evidence and pricing validity, assess operational risks not captured by the toy score, then obtain an authorized procurement approval. The application performs none of those external actions.
